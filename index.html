What changed on October 1, 2026

The SBA's updated rulebook (SOP 50 10 8.1) added a dedicated appendix for every SBA 7(a) change of ownership. If your plan was built on older advice, some of the numbers may have moved.

  1. 4

    Categories. Every change of ownership is classified first: Initial Acquisition, Business Expansion, Owner Buyout, or ESOP & Cooperative. The category sets the equity, cash-flow and documentation requirements.

  2. ½

    The most that seller debt, full-standby debt and minority investors can supply, together, of the required equity.

  3. 1.25×

    Minimum coverage (cash flow divided by yearly debt payments) for initial acquisitions, owner buyouts and ESOPs. It is measured on past results; projections can't be used to meet it.

  4. 10 yrs

    Longest repayment schedule for the business-acquisition portion. A real-estate portion can run up to 25.

  5. 1

    Independent valuation, from an accredited source. At a business purchase price of $3 million or more, initial acquisitions and expansions also need a Quality of Earnings report.

  6. 0

    Earnouts. They remain prohibited. A seller may stay on to consult for up to 24 months in initial acquisitions and expansions.

  7. +

    A working-capital line of credit is now expressly allowed alongside the acquisition financing.

Simplified summary for education, not legal advice. Every deal is subject to lender review.

The honest answers

How much do the buyers need to put in?

It depends on how the deal is classified. A new-owner acquisition generally requires at least 10% equity, and that can't be waived. Owner buyouts also start at 10%, but a lender may reduce it under strict liquidity, working-capital and net-worth conditions.

The buyers are short on equity. Can the seller carry the rest?

Possibly, in part. A seller note never automatically replaces the buyers' own funds. We review the note's amount, standby, payment and lien terms, then tell you what appears supportable and what needs lender approval. SBA rules cap how much of the required equity it can count toward (see What changed on October 1, 2026).

Can I stay on after the sale, or keep a stake?

Often, yes. A transition or consulting role is common. Its maximum length depends on how the deal is classified (see What changed on October 1, 2026). Keeping an ownership stake is different: SBA rules generally treat that as an owner buyout, which requires at least one original owner to remain and personally guarantee the loan. Whether a phased sale fits your goals is a question for you, your attorney and your CPA.

Is an ESOP an option?

It can be. An ESOP buying a controlling interest can qualify for SBA 7(a) financing with special statutory equity treatment, subject to qualification and lender review. Cooperative structures are reviewed case by case.

Will the buyers personally guarantee the loan? What about their homes?

Owners at required ownership levels generally give personal guaranties. That is separate from the SBA's guaranty, which protects the lender. Collateral is reviewed on its own, based on loan size, available assets and lender policy, so no one can promise a home will be excluded before that review.

How long does it take?

SBA acquisition financing goes through full underwriting. Timing depends on the lender, the valuation and how complete the file is. We give you honest best-case and typical ranges for your deal, not a guaranteed date.

Why not go straight to our bank?

You can, and you don't have to leave your bank. But the SBA doesn't make the credit decision: each lender has its own policy, and some don't finance acquisitions. LoanBud works across its lender network to match your file to a policy that fits, which lowers the risk of spending weeks with a lender whose policy never did.

Can the real estate be included?

Often. Real estate the business occupies can be financed alongside the business, subject to occupancy rules, and repaid over a longer period than the business itself (see What changed on October 1, 2026).

What will it cost?

The consultation is free. SBA financing carries lender and program fees that depend on the deal. We separate what can be financed from what must be paid directly, using the current fee schedule, so you see the cost before you commit.

Can you guarantee approval or a closing date?

No, and be cautious of anyone who does. The lender decides after underwriting. What we do is review the evidence early, find problems sooner, and tell you the stage, the open conditions and the next decision at every gate.
